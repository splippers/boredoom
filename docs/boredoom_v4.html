The Concept

One sentence.
One gap in the market.

BoreDOOM! will turn hoovering your house into an AR sport — complete with personal bests, live rivalries, and share cards people actually post.
⚠ Planning Stage
BoreDOOM! is in pre-development. This document describes intended design. The C# codebase is being prototyped by an AI agent sprint team; no playable Quest 3 build exists yet. All screenshots shown are conceptual mockups.

The Problem We're Solving

Everyone with a Meta Quest 3 already exercises in VR. Beat Saber, Supernatural, FitXR — these games prove that people will do physical activity if it's gamified. But those games require dedicated time and setup.

Housework is already happening. You're already moving through space with a tool in your hand. BoreDOOM! wants to intercept that behaviour and turn it into something you look forward to.

The Core Insight

The spatial understanding capabilities of Meta Quest 3 — passthrough, room scanning, mesh tracking — were built for exactly this kind of real-world interaction. No other game has pointed them at the home and said: this is the level.

The app doesn't need the user to go anywhere. The venue is already theirs.

Why Now? Why Quest 3?

  • Full colour passthrough — earlier headsets had greyscale only; Quest 3 makes the real room beautiful inside the HMD
  • Spatial mesh quality — granular enough to track a 25cm coverage grid across an entire floor plan
  • Scene API maturity — OVRSceneRoom/OVRSceneObject are stable enough to build on without hacks
  • Install base — Quest 3 shipped 3M+ units in 2024; the addressable market exists today
  • No direct competition — nobody has built a chore-gamification AR title on any platform
  • Share-card culture — the app store era created users who share game screenshots reflexively

Signature Feature

The 3D Dollhouse

The dollhouse is intended to be the image that defines BoreDOOM! It would work like this: the Meta Scene API provides a floor plan and room boundaries; BoreDOOM! would construct a 1:20 scale miniature of the player's actual home and render it in a corner of the passthrough view — right there, floating in the air, while they hoover around it.

As each room's coverage rises, its miniature counterpart changes colour in real-time: deep red → amber → green → cyan sparkle at full Pristine. The effect is intended to feel like watching a city light up at night — except it's your house, and you're the one turning on the lights.

The "Aha" Moment
We expect the first time a player looks down and sees their own hallway rendered in miniature — and watches it turn green as they pass through it — will be the moment they understand why this game exists.

The Share Card

When a session ends with S or S+ grade — or the player taps the share button — BoreDOOM! would render the dollhouse from directly above at 1080×1080, overlaid with the score, grade, and BoreDOOM! branding. Saved to the Android gallery.

The share card is intended to be the app's organic marketing engine. It needs to look distinctive enough to stop a thumb-scroll.

Game Modes — Expanded

Scan, Sprint & Battle

Chaos Assessment
"Don't clean anything. Just face the truth."
SCAN MODE

Walk through every room without cleaning anything. BoreDOOM! would analyse spatial mesh density — triangles per m² — to estimate clutter levels per room. The dollhouse would fill with fire and smoke effects proportional to each room's chaos score. Results come with roasting commentary.

Planned Chaos Labels
  • 🔥 DOOMED — Score 9+
  • 😬 Chaos Adjacent — Score 7+
  • Lived In (Honest) — Score 5+
  • Mostly Fine — Score 3+
  • 🕵️ Suspiciously Clean — <3
Declutter Dash
"Two minutes. As many items as possible. Go."
TIMED SPRINT

A 2-minute sprint: walk to AR-tagged objects detected by OVRSceneObject classification and "collect" them by stepping within arm's reach. The room would fill with glowing orbs above real furniture and clutter. The timer would turn red inside the final 20 seconds.

50 pts per item
OVRSceneObject API
Timing Options

  • Standard: 120 seconds
  • Marathon: 180 seconds
AR Features Planned

ChoreWars Battle
"Two homes. One network. No excuses."
MULTIPLAYER

Two players on the same WiFi network would each clean their own home simultaneously. Real-time UDP sync would push each player's coverage percentage to the other's HUD, showing YOU'RE WINNING or CATCH UP. First to 80% wins. Margin-based bonus for the victor.

No Internet Needed

Game Modes — Zone Specific

Special Zones

Each zone mode would target a specific high-chaos room type, inheriting full coverage tracking and adding a unique twist mechanic. These are planned for the initial release.

Attic Attack
ZONE

Designed for attic cleaning in near-darkness. Only the "flashlight cone" in front of the player would register as coverage. Cobweb zones require a 2-second dwell to clear. Skip more than 20% and a Darkness Penalty (0.8×) would reduce the final score. Looking up earns ceiling bonus points.

Cobweb zones8 per session
Ceiling bonusup to +200 pts
Darkness penalty0.8× if <80% cobwebs
Shed Dread
ZONE

Shed organisation concept: 10 AR tool icons would float around the real shed. Pick each up (1.5s dwell) and file in the correct colour-coded zone. Correct placements build a combo multiplier; misplacements cost 20 pts. Random spider encounters spawn for shoo-away bonus points.

Correct placement50 × combo pts
Misplacement−20 pts
Spider bonus+10 pts each
Basement Bust
ZONE

Designed for basements. AR would highlight damp patches and stains detected in the spatial mesh. Each stain zone would require extended dwell time to "treat." Coverage would count double in low-ceiling zones, with a bonus for clearing all stain zones before finishing standard coverage.

Stain zones
2× low-ceiling coverage

ZONE

For the garage. AR would mark oil stain zones requiring double-passes. A coverage bonus for cleaning behind detected large objects (cars, shelving). Tight-corridor navigation between virtual walls would earn precision bonuses. Grease and grime, gamified.

Viral Potential

Why This Could Go Viral

"Nobody has ever made housework feel like a sport. The hardware to do it has existed for eighteen months. We just have to build the game."
01
The Share Card
Every session could end with an auto-generated overhead shot of your dollhouse — heat-mapped, graded, timestamped. The hypothesis: it's distinctive enough that people post it without being asked, and their friends ask what app it is.
02
Floor Art
Tracing a geometric pattern with the hoover, then photographing the overhead dollhouse view, would produce content that looks like carpet crop circles. The TikTok caption writes itself: "I drew a diamond in my living room with the hoover."
03
Chaos Assessment
Showing someone their chaos score would provoke one of two reactions — outrage or pride. "My kitchen scored 8.9 DOOMED 🔥" is exactly the kind of shareable sentence that drives app discovery. The roast comments would write themselves.
04
ChoreWars Battle
Two people in different homes racing to clean simultaneously, with a live score ticker, would produce genuine urgency. "My partner reached 80% while I was still finding the cable" is the kind of story people tell, and it ends with "what app was it?"
05
Ghost Run
Chasing your own ghost while hoovering is inherently absurd and funny to explain. "I'm racing myself. My past self is faster." Fitness apps have proven that self-competition drives extraordinary retention — and this is a brand-new context for it.
06
The Dollhouse Moment
We expect that watching your own house render in miniature in the corner of your AR view — rooms turning from red to green as you clean — will be described as "magic" by first-time users. That word, shared person-to-person, is the most reliable distribution channel there is.
Target Audience

Primary: 18–35 Quest 3 owners already using VR fitness apps (Beat Saber, Supernatural, FitXR). They're pre-conditioned to gamified physical activity and already own the hardware. Secondary: productivity-gamification users (Habitica, Streaks). The insight: these people already feel guilty about not keeping the house clean. BoreDOOM! is intended to resolve that cognitive dissonance — and make them feel good about it.

Landscape & Monetisation

Competitive Landscape

The Gap

BoreDOOM! is the first intended product to combine passthrough AR, real-home spatial tracking, and gamified housework. The gap exists because the hardware to do it properly — Meta Quest 3's colour passthrough and mesh quality — only became available in late 2023.
